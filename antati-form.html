Tamil / Antāti

அந்தாதி (antāti) is a portmanteau — antam ("end") + āti ("beginning") — for a Tamil verse form with one generative rule: the last word (or syllable) of each verse becomes the first word of the next. Tradition credits the Shaiva saint Karaikal Ammaiyar with composing the first one; by Kambar's time (12th century) it was common enough to be used for poems in praise of almost anything. Seven of this site's eight Tamil chants — Abirami Antati, Arpudha Tiruvantati, Kanninun Cirutampu, Saraswati Antati, and the Mudhal, Irandam, and Munram Tiruvantati that open the Naalayira Divya Prabandham's Iyaṟpā section — are antātis; Vel Maaral, despite also being a "garland" (māl/mālai) of verses, uses a different mechanism (a repeated refrain, not a word-chain — see its own structural note) and isn't one.

The rule in practice

Every antāti on this site literally does this, verse after verse — not approximately, the actual closing word recurs, sometimes with a small case/sandhi change:

Abirami Antati
…விழுத்துணையே.
…vizhuth-thuNaiyE. (v.1 ends)
துணையும்…
thuNaiyum… (v.2 opens)
Arpudha Tiruvantati
…தீர்ப்ப திடர்.
…thīrppa thidar. (v.1 ends)
இடர்களையா…
idarkaḷaiyā… (v.2 opens)
Kanninun Cirutampu
…என் நாவுக்கே
…en nAvukkE (v.1 ends)
நாவினால்…
nAvinAl… (v.2 opens)
Saraswati Antati
…தாளை வணங்குதுமே.
…thAlai vaNangudhumE. (v.1 ends)
வணங்குஞ்…
vaNanguñ… (v.2 opens)

The Iyaṟpā trilogy (no Tamil-script source found for these three yet — see their own colophon notes) chains identically in romanization:

Mudhal Tiruvantati …idarAzhi nIngugavE enRu. (v.1 ends) → enrU kadal kadaindhadhu… (v.2 opens)
Irandam Tiruvantati …gyAnath thamizh purindha nAn. (v.1 ends) → gyAnaththAl nangu uNarndhu… (v.2 opens)
Munram Tiruvantati …vaNNan pAL inRu. (v.1 ends) → inRE kazhal kaNdEn… (v.2 opens)

A closed garland, not just a chain

Wikipedia's Antati article notes that "in some instances" the very last verse loops back to open the first one, turning the whole poem into a closed ring rather than a line — mālai, "garland," in the fullest sense. That's not a rare exception among this site's antātis; it's every single one of them, including five whose scale (100+ verses) would make a coincidence very unlikely:

Abirami Antati (100 v.)
உதிக்கின்ற…
udhikkinRa… (v.1 opens)
…உதிக்கின்றவே.
…udhikkinRavE. (v.100 ends)
Arpudha Tiruvantati (101 v.)
பிறந்து…
piRandhu… (v.1 opens)
…காதல் பிறந்து.
…kAdhal piRandhu. (v.101 ends)
Saraswati Antati (30 v.)
சீர்தந்த…
sIrthantha… (v.1 opens)
…பெருஞ் சீர்தருமே.
…perum sIrtharumE. (v.30 ends)

Kanninun Cirutampu's own closure is softer — it opens கண்ணி (kaNNi) and closes …காண்மினே (…kANminE), sharing a root sound rather than a repeated word — a reminder that the "rule" tolerates near-rhyme at the edges of a poem the way it doesn't mid-poem. The trilogy (100 verses each) closes just as tightly, in romanization:

Mudhal Tiruvantati vaiyam thagaLiyA… (v.1 opens) → …mAyavanaiyE manaththu vai. (v.100 ends)
Irandam Tiruvantati anbE thagaLiyA ArvamE… (v.1 opens) → …aLavanRAl yAnudaiya anbu. (v.100 ends)
Munram Tiruvantati thirukkaNdEn ponmEni… (v.1 opens) → …thEnamarum pUmEl thiru. (v.100 ends)
