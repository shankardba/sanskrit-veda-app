Not full vowels, but signs riding on one: anusvāra nasalises the vowel into a following consonant's place; chandrabindu is a lighter, more open nasalisation (as in हँस, "swan") — this site transliterates both as ṃ, same as the chant pages; visarga is a light voiceless breath after the vowel; avagraha marks a dropped अ in sandhi.

Two very rare Vedic-only marks — jihvāmūlīya and upadhmānīya (breath before क/ख and प/फ) — are left out for now since they don't occur anywhere in this site's chants, and the fonts in use here don't render them cleanly. They can be added later if a chant that uses them is.

Each decade is its own word (not a compound of its digit), and falls into one of two families by ending: -ति/-ti (viṃśati, ṣaṣṭi, saptati, aśīti, navati) or -त्/-t (triṃśat, catvāriṃśat, pañcāśat) — both are grammatically feminine nouns, declined like ordinary nouns of those endings rather than like the digits themselves.

Building 21–99

Sanskrit doesn't have 78 separate words for 21–99 to memorise — almost all of them are the unit digit fused onto its decade, by one of two rules:

Additive (the default)

unit-prefix + decade
द्वा + विंशति = द्वाविंशति (dvā + viṃśati = dvāviṃśati) — 22
सप्त + शत = सप्तशत (sapta + śata = saptaśata) — 700

Subtractive (numbers ending in 9)

ekona- ("one less") + next decade
एकोन + त्रिंशत् = एकोनत्रिंशत् (ekona + triṃśat = ekonatriṃśat) — 29
एकोन + शत = एकोनशत (ekona + śata = ekonaśata) — 99

लक्ष (lakṣa, "lakh") and कोटि (koṭi, "crore") are still everyday units across South Asia today. निर्युत (niyuta) is sometimes swapped with प्रयुत (prayuta) for 10⁶ — sources disagree on the exact order there. Beyond शङ्ख (10¹³), different traditions (Vedic, Puranic, and later Buddhist texts like the Lalitavistāra) each give their own, mutually inconsistent names for still-larger powers of ten — rather than present one as definitive, this page stops at the point where the sources still agree.

From 5 (पञ्च) through 19, there's a single indeclinable-by-gender plural form — only the case changes, same word for masculine/feminine/neuter alike. The decades (20–100) are themselves feminine singular nouns, grammatically a collective ("a-twenty," "a-hundred") rather than a count word, with whatever's being counted usually going into the genitive plural alongside it.
