Lineages

Guru to disciple, down to the present day — and where the lines touch.

A paramparā (परम्परा, "one after another") is a chain of teachers, each passing on what they received. This tree starts from Ādi Śaṅkara, whom tradition credits with organising renunciates into ten named orders, and follows three of those branches down to the present: the Purī line through Totāpurī to Ramakrishna, Vivekananda and Swami Sarvapriyananda; the Giri line of Sri Yukteswar and Paramahansa Yogananda, whose Kriyā Yoga came separately through Babaji and Lahiri Mahasaya; and the Sarasvatī line of Swami Sivananda and Swami Chinmayananda, who also studied under Tapovan Maharaj. Solid lines are teacher to disciple, dotted lines are order membership, and the coloured links show where the branches meet. Select anyone to see who they learned from and who they taught.

Between the lineages

The branches share a root but never merged. They cross at one person and run in parallel at several points.
