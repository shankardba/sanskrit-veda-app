Lineages

Guru to disciple, down to the present day — and where the lines touch.

A paramparā (परम्परा, "one after another") is a chain of teachers, each passing on what they received. At its centre is Ādi Śaṅkara, who learned from Govinda Bhagavatpāda, who learned from Gauḍapāda; above them runs the legendary chain back to Vyāsa and Nārāyaṇa. Tradition credits Śaṅkara with organising renunciates into ten named orders, and the tree follows three of those branches down to the present: the Purī line through Totāpurī to Ramakrishna, Vivekananda and Swami Sarvapriyananda; the Giri line of Sri Yukteswar and Paramahansa Yogananda, whose Kriyā Yoga came separately through Babaji and Lahiri Mahasaya and divides after him into Self-Realization Fellowship and Swami Kriyananda's Ananda; and the Sarasvatī line of Swami Sivananda and Swami Chinmayananda, who also studied under Tapovan Maharaj. A fourth branch, Ramana Maharshi, took no order and had no human guru. It joins the root by teaching, not ordination, and reaches the present through Papaji to Gangaji and Mooji. Beside Śaṅkara stands a separate root, the Nāth yogis of Matsyendranāth and Gorakhnāth (not Śaṅkara's teachers: a different Śaiva order, centuries later). From the Nāth tradition descends the householder Inchegiri line of Siddharameshwar and Nisargadatta Maharaj, whose I Am That was recorded by a devotee of Ramana. Between the lineages

The branches share a root but never merged. They cross in a few documented meetings and run in parallel at several points.
